Shabtai Gabbai Chacham Dayan

Question

Where did the idea that David conquered Jerusalem "single handedly" come from?

I saw a clip by R' Shlomo Carlebach where he claimed that Jerusalem is "David's possession" because he conquered it single handedly. This seems to go against basic Tanach, as well as Midrash (e.g. the Midrash about him lifting Yoav up on a tree etc.)

Nonetheless, Reb Shlomo knew his stuff so I want to give him the benefit of the doubt - whence this idea?

Answer

The supported origin of the idea is not that David had no help at all, but that the Tanakh explicitly credits him with the capture of the stronghold: “David captured the stronghold of Zion” and “David occupied the stronghold.”. That wording can easily become homiletic language that Jerusalem is “David’s possession,” even though the same verses also say that David’s men were with him and that Joab was the first to attack and later rebuild parts of the city. [1] [2]

Abarbanel makes the strongest explicit claim in that direction. He says that the verse means David himself, because of his bravery, captured the fortress first, and that it was therefore called by his name rather than Joab’s. He then uses Chronicles to refine the story: David commanded the capture, Joab went up first, and Joab received the promised rank. So Abarbanel is not saying David had literally no participant around him; he is emphasizing that the city is credited to David. [3]

Chronicles also helps explain where the “single-handed” flavor comes from. It juxtaposes “David and all Israel” going to Jerusalem with the statement that David himself captured the citadel, while separately saying Joab went up first and later “restored” the rest of the city. In other words, the text assigns the conquest to David as king, while acknowledging Joab’s tactical role. [1]

Samuel adds another layer: David speaks as the leader who sets the terms for the assault, promising rank to “whoever attacks the Jebusites first.”. That is the opposite of a claim that nobody else was involved, but it does show that David is the one who frames and owns the victory narrative. [2]

Rabbeinu Bahya shows a later rabbinic move in the same direction: he cites a Midrash on “David captured the stronghold of Zion, which is the City of David,” and takes the name as a theological statement about why Jerusalem bears David’s name. That supports a symbolic reading of “David’s possession,” though it still does not say he personally acted entirely alone. [4]

Josephus likewise is not “single-handed” in the literal sense: he says David and the people came to Jerusalem, that David laid siege, and that Joab was the one who first got into the citadel. This is another witness that the classical tradition can credit David with the conquest while still recognizing the participation of others. [9]

So the best-supported answer is: the phrase likely comes from a homiletic reading of “David captured the stronghold” in Samuel and Chronicles, reinforced by Abarbanel and related interpretations that stress David’s ownership of the conquest and the city’s being named for him. It is not supported as a literal historical claim that he acted with no assistance whatsoever. [1] [3]

Still open

  • No source here identifies the exact sermon, midrash, or oral tradition behind Reb Shlomo Carlebach’s wording.
  • No source here proves that anyone classically said David conquered Jerusalem absolutely alone in the historical sense.

Our sages commented on Samuel II 5,7 where we read וילכוד דוד את מצודת ציון היא עיר דוד, “David captured the citadel of David which became the city of David.” The Midrash asks why the city was called after a mortal, i.e. after King David? The answer provided is that G’d knew perfectly well that the city would initially be built and subsequently destroyed by mortal human beings. This is why during the periods preceding the final Temple, Jerusalem was known as the city of David, i.e. the city of a mortal human being. However, in the future, it will bear the name of G’d Himself seeing that it would then no longer be subject to destruction. This is the meaning of Isaiah 60,14 והלכו אליך שחוח בני מעניך והשתחוו....וקראו לך עיר ה' ציון קדוש ישראל, “they shall come before you, the children of those who have tormented you....and you will be called ‘city of the Lord, Zion, the Holy One of Israel.”

[8] What are the "blind and the lame" that David wanted to be rid of when he entered Jerusalem, and what does that mean?

miyodeya · Alex

As you said, Ralbag (and most of the commentaries) understand this to be talking about statues of some kind. (Metzudas David to 5:8 also cites this as a second explanation.) So according to that view of things, David had nothing against the blind and lame people any more than against any of the other Jebusites. Metzudas David's first explanation (to 5:6), though, is indeed that it means people suffering from these handicaps. However, he explains the mention of them as part of the Jebusites' boast: "Our tower [this is his explanation of צנור in that verse] is so strongly fortified that even if its only remaining defenders were the blind and the lame, you would still be unable to conquer it!" So David's challenge to his men (v. 8) is: "Disprove their claim! First topple the tower while it is still defended by all of the Jebusite army, and only then deal with these insolent Jebusites - including the blind and lame people, all of whom had joined in jeering us."

[11] How long did King David reign in Jerusalem before he brought the Ark there?

miyodeya · Harel13

According to Samuel 1:7:2 , the Ark was in Kiryath-jearim for 20 years. According to Seder Olam Rabbah , this period encompasses the ca. 11 years of Shmuel's time as judge, ca. two years of Shaul as king, and ca. 7.5 years of David as king in Hebron. After 7.5 years in Hebron David conquered Jerusalem ( Samuel 2:5:4-9 ), so that means that shortly after that, sometime into his eighth year, he made his first attempt at bringing the Ark. That attempt failed, and the Ark waited another three months before David's second attempt ( Samuel 2:6:11 ; Chronicles 1:13:14 ), which is the one you're asking about. This likely occurred around the end of David's eighth year. So it seems that David ruled in Jerusalem for less than a year or about a year before the Ark was brought there.
