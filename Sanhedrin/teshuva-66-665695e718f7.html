Question

Why wasn't the water in the bath and lavers invalidated overnight?

In Zevachim 20-21 there is a discussion regarding the water in the Kiyor, with which the kohanim would wash their hands and feet, and whether or not the water would become invalidated (pasul) if left uncovered overnight (linah) as a sort of extension of the korbanot it was helping the Kohanim prepare for.

Rambam (pesulei mukdashim 19:3) rules that it would be pasul.

I would like to understand why the same pasul would/not extend to the Yam (bath) and lavers Shlomo made (1 Kings 7). I am not aware of any discussion regarding these items and their water being invalidated if left uncovered overnight.

Answer

The Yerushalmi/Rambam-based source says the Yam served as a bath for the kohanim. [2]

The Kiyor itself is a sacred vessel. [1]

The ten lavers were “for washing” the burnt-offering parts. The Yam was “for the priests to wash in”. [5]

It was connected to a natural water source. [2]

They tell us only what they were used for. And where they stood. But not whether their water had the Kiyor’s sanctity-status or a different one. [5]

The lavers’ overnight status is not explicitly addressed here. [5]

Still open

  • An explicit source in the provided materials that discusses overnight invalidation for Solomon’s ten lavers themselves, separate from the Kiyor and the Yam.
  • An explicit source stating that the lavers’ water was or was not treated exactly like the Kiyor’s water overnight.
  • The provided sources explicitly support the Yam’s different status, but they do not explicitly address the ten lavers’ overnight invalidation.
  • Paragraphs 1, 2, 4, and 6 rely on inference for the Yam distinction; that inference is supported, but the exact line of comparison to the Kiyor is not stated verbatim in one source.
